# ADR 0003 — Should MyHabits move to a Backend-as-a-Service?

- **Status:** proposed
- **Date:** 2026-09-13
- **Deciders:** the owner (single-user project)
- **Related:** [ADR 0001 — Where the data lives](0001-data-backend.md),
  [ADR 0002 — Reminders](0002-reminders.md) and its 2026-09 amendment
- **Relationship to ADR 0001:** ADR 0001 is not wrong; it was decided under
  constraints that no longer hold. §1.1 states which ones moved. Anything below
  that contradicts ADR 0001 does so deliberately and says why.

The owner asked, verbatim: *« est-ce que ça résout : la dispo/SLA ? les backups,
le versionning, la sécu, l'authent utilisateur ? »* — about Supabase and
comparable platforms. This ADR answers those five, in that order, without
hedging, and then decides.

## 1. Context

### 1.1 The constraints moved three times in a week

ADR 0001 was decided on 2026-09-05 under a set of drivers that has since been
demolished, mostly by the owner himself. Deciding this question against ADR
0001's drivers would produce a confident wrong answer.

| # | Today's constraint | What it kills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **N1** | **The project is closed source and has exactly one user.** | ADR 0001's **D1** ("the maintainer must not be able to see the data"). Already formally retired by ADR 0002's amendment §A.1: *"the server operator and the data subject are the same person."* D1 cannot be used to reject anything in this document. |
| **N2** | *« j'ai pas envie de configurer autant d'infra »* — the larger GCP build was rejected. | Anything that trades one problem for a console full of resources. For calibration, that build is **418 lines of Terraform** in `infra/` plus Cloud Run, Cloud Scheduler, GCS, Secret Manager and a service account. |
| **N3** | **Degoogling.** Ideally *one long-running Docker image on his own server*, behind a reverse proxy that terminates **HTTPS but does not authenticate**. | The status quo. Google Sheets does not lose this document on a metric — it loses it on a **requirement**. Firebase and everything Google-owned are out of scope by the same clause. |
| **N4** | **Someone else should be responsible for durability and auth.** | Naive self-hosting, and hand-rolled login. |

**N3 and N4 point in opposite directions, and that is the whole decision.**
"Run it myself on my own box" and "let someone else own durability and auth" are
two halves of a contradiction. Supabase Cloud takes N2 + N4 and most of N3 (not
Google, but not his server either). PocketBase and the plain container take N3
and most of N2, and fail N4 outright. Supabase self-hosted takes N3 and fails N2
and N4 at once, which is why it is the worst option in this document.

### 1.3 ADR 0001's decisive argument has already been half-spent

ADR 0001 rejected every alternative largely on **D4**: leaving the spreadsheet
means building config CRUD for 36 columns, priced at *"credibly 800–1 500 lines,
permanently, for no user-visible feature."*

That argument is measurably weaker today, because **the goals editor was built
anyway**, for UX reasons, while staying on Sheets:

| Component | Lines | Columns covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `src/ui/components/GoalEditor.tsx` | 397 | 16 (`Goals`) |
| `src/ui/components/GoalsPanel.tsx` | 188 | — |
| `PromoteNote` in `JournalScreen.tsx` | ~150 | a subset of the 17 `Config` columns |
| **Already spent** | **~735** | |

So the 800–1 500-line bill is not hypothetical future work — **roughly 735 of it
is already written and merged, and leaving Sheets did not cause it.** What
remains unbuilt is a `Config` editor (17 columns, including the `schedule`
mini-language, `depends_on`, `options[]`, `colors[]`) and `Tags` (3). At the
observed ~37 lines per column that is **~600–750 lines, not 1 500**. D4 is still
a real cost; it is no longer a veto.

### 1.4 How the configuration is actually edited — and the fact that changes trap 1

The most load-bearing observation in this document, and it is not in ADR 0001.

He does not do CRUD. `perso/generate.py` **generates** `config.tsv`, `tags.tsv`
and `goals.tsv`, and its header says: *"Re-run this after editing, then paste each
.tsv into the matching tab of the spreadsheet."* That is how 30 metrics, 9 goals
and 8 tags got in. Two consequences:

1. **What he needs from an admin UI is bulk paste of a generated TSV, not a form
   per row.** An option offering 36 columns of hand-editing and no bulk import
   solves the wrong problem.
2. **`perso/` is in `.gitignore`** — *"Personal configuration — never committed."*
   So the config is **not** under version control today, and the only history of
   what his metric definitions used to be is **Google Drive's revision history of
   the spreadsheet**. That is the answer to question 3, in §3.3.

## 2. Decision drivers

Ordered. The first three dominate.

| # | Driver | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D1** | **Fewest things that can be silently broken while he is not looking.** | The failure mode that matters is not downtime, it is a reminder that quietly stops arriving or a backup that quietly stops running. |
| **D2** | **Auth must not be his own invention exposed to the internet.** | N4. The proxy does not authenticate. This is the one place laziness is not acceptable. |
| **D3** | **No Google in the data path.** | N3, stated. |
| **D4** | **Bulk TSV in, TSV out — the `generate.py` workflow survives.** | §1.4. |
| **D5** | **Scheduled conditional push keeps working**, ideally reusing `server/decide.js`, which is pure and already covered by `tests/push.test.ts`. | ADR 0002 is done; re-deriving it is a regression. |
| **D6** | **~€0/month**, or a number small enough not to think about. | Stated in ADR 0001, unchanged. |
| **D7** | **Durability of a multi-year health record.** | Worst possible outcome. |
| **D8** | **Lines of UI I would have to write that deliver no feature.** | §1.3. |

Absent on purpose, and worth naming so nobody re-imports them: scale,
concurrency, query expressiveness, multi-tenant isolation, realtime. A BaaS is
excellent at all five. This app needs none of them.

## 3. The five questions

### 3.1 Availability and SLA — **no, and none of them is worth paying for**

First, the distinction he is really asking about: an **uptime promise** is
marketing; a **credit-backed SLA** is a contract that pays out when it is
breached.

| Platform | Credit-backed SLA? | Starts at | Pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supabase | Yes | **Enterprise only** — not Free, not Pro ($25), not Team ($599) | 99.9 %/month; 10 % / 15 % / 20 % / 30 % of monthly fees as **billing credit**, capped at 20 % of annual fees |
| Appwrite | Yes | **Enterprise** | Not published |
| Nhost | Email-support SLA on Team ($599); uptime SLA on Enterprise | **Team / Enterprise** | Not published |
| Directus | Not found on Core or Team; Enterprise is contact-sales | — | NOT VERIFIED |
| PocketBase, plain container | No | — | — |
| Google Sheets (his current store) | **No** — Workspace's 99.9 % SLA with 3/7/15-day service credits applies to *paid Workspace customers*, not to a personal account | — | — |

([supabase.com/sla](https://supabase.com/sla), [supabase.com/pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing),
[appwrite.io/pricing](https://appwrite.io/pricing), [nhost.io/pricing](https://nhost.io/pricing),
[directus.com/pricing](https://directus.com/pricing),
[workspace.google.com/terms/sla](https://workspace.google.com/terms/sla/) — all checked 2026-09-13.)

**Answer: no BaaS he would plausibly buy carries an SLA. He would have to reach
Supabase Enterprise, and the payout would be a discount on the bill he would not
otherwise be paying.**

Now proportion. One person fills one form once a day, and the PWA is already
offline-tolerant by construction: `useTracker` applies answers to local state
immediately, persists on a 700 ms debounce, returns failed days to a pending set,
and flushes on `visibilitychange`. An hour of backend downtime costs a delayed
write, not a lost answer and not money. **An SLA is not worth paying for here at
any price.** The Supabase Free footnote that does matter is not the missing SLA
but the pause after *"low activity over a 7-day period"*
([free-project-pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing),
checked 2026-09-13) — and, correcting ADR 0001's harsher reading, **resuming is a
one-click self-serve button**, available for about a year. Annoying after a
fortnight's holiday. Not disqualifying.

### 3.2 Backups — **no on the free tier, and the free tier is worse than what he has**

| Tier | What is taken | Retention | Self-serve restore | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supabase **Free** | **Nothing automatic.** Docs: *"We recommend that free tier plan projects regularly export their data using the Supabase CLI `db dump` command and maintain off-site backups."* | — | n/a | [backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups) |
| Supabase **Pro** $25/mo | Daily logical backups | **7 days** | Yes, dashboard | same |
| Supabase **Team** $599/mo | Daily | 14 days | Yes | same |
| **PITR** | Add-on, Pro and above only | 7 / 14 / 28 days | Yes | **+$100 / +$200 / +$400 per month** |
| Appwrite Pro $25/mo | Daily | 7 days | Yes | [appwrite.io/pricing](https://appwrite.io/pricing) |
| Nhost Pro $25/mo | Automated + PITR | 7 days | Yes | [nhost.io/pricing](https://nhost.io/pricing) |
| **PocketBase** | ZIP of `pb_data`, on a **cron expression**, local or **S3**, `CronMaxKeep` retention | your choice | **Yes, from the admin UI** | [going-to-production](https://pocketbase.io/docs/going-to-production/) |
| Plain container | `sqlite.backup()` / `VACUUM INTO` on a cron, or Litestream streaming to S3 | your choice | `cp` | [sqlite.org/backup](https://sqlite.org/backup.html), [lang_vacuum](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_vacuum.html), [litestream.io](https://litestream.io) |

The framing that matters: **the app can already dump everything as TSV**
(`tabular.ts` is the canonical row format and both adapters speak it). So the
question is not "can I get my data out" but "what does the platform add on top of
a dump I could cron myself?" On Supabase Free, nothing — it adds a homework
assignment. On Pro, $300/year buys 7 days of dailies and a restore button; a
`VACUUM INTO` piped to any object store is five lines and gives retention he
chooses.

**Answer: no, a BaaS does not solve backups unless he pays, and even then it
matches rather than beats a cron.** PocketBase is the only option here that ships
a genuinely good backup story for free.

### 3.3 Versioning — **no, and this is the one that is uncomfortable**

Say it plainly: **Google Sheets is the only option in this document that gives
row-level history, and it gives it for free, automatically, with a human-readable
diff and a restore button.** Everything he is considering is a regression here.

- **PostgreSQL has no built-in SQL:2011 system-versioned temporal tables** — the
  design is on the wiki, unmerged ([SQL2011Temporal](https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/SQL2011Temporal)).
  **`temporal_tables` is not on Supabase's published extension list** (the page
  says "over 50" without enumerating, so absence is not proven, but there is no
  evidence of it). Supabase's own answer is [`supa_audit`](https://github.com/supabase/supa_audit) —
  trigger-based, i.e. **you install and own it**.
- **Backups are not versioning.** A 7-day daily snapshot cannot tell you what the
  `colors` cell of `coucher` said in March.
- **PocketBase has no row history.** A `_audit` collection written from a hook.
- **Plain container:** a SQLite `AFTER UPDATE`/`AFTER DELETE` trigger writing old
  rows into an `audit` table is ~**20 lines of SQL** and keeps forever.

Now the part that shrinks the problem. **What actually needs history?**

| Table | Needs it? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Entries` | **No** | Append-only-with-tombstones by ADR 0001 §7.1, every row carrying `updated_at`. Corrections are new rows; `typeEntries` collapses to the effective answer. History *is* the storage format. |
| `Goals` | **No** | Versions itself by design — `supersede()` in `src/core/goals.ts`, and the repository contract says so: *"the caller closes the current goal with a `to` date and saves a new one, so the history of what the bar used to be survives."* |
| `Notes`, `Events`, `Tags` | **No** | Author-edited prose and cosmetics. |
| **`Config`** | **Yes** | 30 rows. Renaming a `scale` level or reordering `colors` **silently reinterprets a decade of stored values**, because entries store the level *label*. Losing history here means losing the meaning of old data. |

So the exposure is **one table, ~30 rows, changing a handful of times a year** —
and it is the table *generated by a script*.

**The lazy fix is not a database feature. It is `git init` in `perso/`.**
`generate.py` and its three TSVs are the source of truth for `Config`; they are
currently `.gitignore`d into oblivion. Committing them to a private repo (or any
repo on his own server) gives him a better history than Drive did — authored,
diffable, with a message — for zero lines of application code, and it works
identically under *every* option in this document including the status quo.

**Answer: no BaaS solves versioning. Google Sheets did. But the only table where
it matters is generated from a file he can version with `git`, which costs
nothing and is independent of the backend choice. Do that regardless of what is
decided below.**

### 3.4 Security — **RLS is the strongest thing on the table, and it is solving a problem he does not have**

**What RLS actually is,** concretely, because it deserves a fair hearing. A
policy is a Postgres object attached to a table:

```sql
alter table entries enable row level security;
create policy "own rows" on entries for all
  using (auth.uid() = user_id) with check (auth.uid() = user_id);
```

Postgres injects that predicate into **every** query against the table, whatever
the path — PostgREST, the SQL editor, psql, a third-party tool. It is not a check
in application code that a forgotten route can skip; it is the database refusing.
The docs warn explicitly that *"a table in an exposed schema without RLS is
readable and writable by any role with a grant on it"*
([RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security),
checked 2026-09-13), and `service_role` carries `bypassrls` and must never reach a
browser.

Now weigh it. **There is one user.** The set of rows RLS isolates him from is
empty. Its real job here should be named honestly: **RLS exists to make a
publicly published key safe.** The Supabase model is browser → Postgres directly,
which requires shipping an `anon` key in the JS bundle, world-readable. RLS is
the gate between that key and the data. So the ledger reads: **gain** a
database-enforced authorisation layer and a battle-tested auth service (§3.5);
**cost** a public credential that does not exist today, whose safety depends on
never creating a table in the exposed schema without enabling RLS — one forgotten
checkbox between "private" and "a health journal on the open internet".

Against the status quo that is a **net regression**: today there is no long-lived
credential anywhere. The OAuth token is memory-only, scoped to `drive.file`, and
dies with the tab (ADR 0001 §3.5) — a stronger posture than any option below it.

**Secret handling and attack surface, per option:**

| Option | Secrets he must hold | Processes exposed to the network |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supabase Cloud | `anon` key (public, in the bundle), `service_role` key (never client-side), DB password | 0 — theirs |
| Supabase self-hosted | JWT secret, anon + service keys, Postgres password, dashboard credentials, SMTP credentials, TLS | **7 core containers**: Studio, Kong/Envoy, Auth (GoTrue), PostgREST, Realtime, Storage, Postgres — plus optional Logflare + Vector ([self-hosting/docker](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting/docker), checked 2026-09-13) |
| PocketBase | admin password, OAuth client secrets, SMTP, VAPID key | **1** static Go binary, ~15 MB, no interpreter |
| Plain container | session secret (or a bcrypt hash in the Caddyfile), VAPID private key | **1** `node:24-alpine` + `web-push` (5 transitive deps) |

On a personal box behind a reverse proxy, **the number of daemons that need
patching is the security metric that actually predicts incidents**, and
self-hosted Supabase loses that contest 9 to 1.

**Answer: RLS is genuinely excellent engineering and genuinely close to moot
here. Adopting Supabase does not improve his security posture; it changes its
shape and adds a public key. The self-hosted variant makes it materially worse.**

### 3.5 User authentication — **yes. This is the one question a BaaS actually answers.**

His reverse proxy terminates TLS and does not authenticate, so today the app
would be open to anyone who finds the hostname. This is the real problem.

| | Supabase (GoTrue) | PocketBase | Plain container, hand-rolled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email + password | Yes | Yes | ~40 lines `crypto.scrypt` + `timingSafeEqual` |
| Magic link / OTP | Yes | Email OTP yes; magic link NOT VERIFIED as distinct | No |
| OAuth providers | **19** built-in + custom OIDC ([social-login](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/social-login)) | **30** built-in ([`tools/auth`](https://github.com/pocketbase/pocketbase/tree/master/tools/auth)) | No |
| MFA | **Basic TOTP included on Free** through Enterprise. Phone/WhatsApp MFA is $75/mo, not on Free ([pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)) | Yes, since v0.23 | No |
| Sessions + refresh | Handled by `supabase-js` | Stateless HS256 JWT; **no server-side revocation list** — "logout" is discarding the token | ~30 lines HMAC-signed `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite` cookie |
| Login rate limiting | Published defaults: **30 sign-in requests / 5 min / IP**, 150 token requests / 5 min, 60 s OTP cooldown ([rate-limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits)) | Built-in rate limiter since v0.23.0 | **None unless written** |
| Built-in email | **2 emails/hour** on the shared SMTP — bring your own SMTP for real use | `sendmail` by default; docs "highly recommend" external SMTP | n/a |

Two honest notes, cutting in opposite directions.

**For the BaaS:** these are the only implementations here that have been attacked
by strangers for years. His 100 lines have been attacked by nobody. A bug in
hand-rolled session handling is silent and the blast radius is a decade of health
data. Hand-rolled auth exposed to the internet is the one place laziness is not
acceptable.

**Against over-stating it:** the 2 emails/hour cap that would strangle a real
product is irrelevant at n=1, and the hand-rolled version on the table is better
supported by the standard library than "hand-rolled auth" usually implies —
`crypto.scrypt`, `crypto.timingSafeEqual`, `crypto.createHmac`, three cookie
flags. What actually goes wrong is not the crypto but the **missing rate
limiter** (online guessing against an endpoint with no WAF).

**The lazy answer that removes the risk entirely, and costs zero lines:**

```caddyfile
myhabits.example.org {
    basic_auth {
        vbo $2a$14$...   # caddy hash-password
    }
    reverse_proxy localhost:8080
}
```

A built-in Caddy directive, bcrypt by default, hash from `caddy hash-password`
([docs](https://caddyserver.com/docs/caddyfile/directives/basic_auth), checked
2026-09-13; renamed from `basicauth` in v2.8.0). Traefik has the equivalent. His
constraint said the proxy *does not* authenticate — that is a configuration, not a
law. The cost: HTTP Basic has no logout, no French error copy, no MFA, and shows a
browser dialog rather than a screen — ugly on a phone, mildly awkward for an
installed PWA. The benefit: **the one question a BaaS answers, answered correctly
in ten minutes, with no vendor and no code.**

**Answer: yes, a BaaS solves authentication, completely and well — the strongest
argument in favour of one. It is not the only correct answer at one user, and the
alternatives are a Caddyfile line or ~100 reviewable lines of stdlib.**

## 4. Trap 1 — who edits the configuration

The app is config-driven: 17 `Config` columns + 3 `Tags` + 16 `Goals` = 36. The
workflow is §1.4: `generate.py` → TSV → paste. **So the question is not "is there
a CRUD UI", it is "can I paste 30 rows of TSV into it, from a phone?"**

| Option | Table editor | Bulk CSV/TSV import | Usable on a phone | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Google Sheets (today)** | The best one that exists | Paste, natively | Yes, the Sheets app | The bar. Nothing below clears it. |
| **Supabase Studio** | Yes, spreadsheet-like grid, `Insert → Import Data from CSV` ([import-data](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/import-data)) | **Yes** — ~100 MB cap (SEMI-VERIFIED: corroborated by secondary sources, not quoted from an official page). Larger → `pgloader`/`COPY` | **NOT VERIFIED.** No official statement either way; it is a data-grid-heavy dashboard and should be assumed desktop-first | Good on desktop. Unknown on the device the app is used on. |
| **PocketBase admin** | Yes, record tables | **No.** Record-level CSV import was requested and **closed as "not planned"** ([issue #48](https://github.com/pocketbase/pocketbase/issues/48)). Only collection *schema* JSON import exists | **No.** A responsive-layout proposal was marked out of roadmap: *"hard to manage via mobile… not its main goal"* ([issue #3803](https://github.com/pocketbase/pocketbase/issues/3803)) | **Fails both halves.** This is the finding that demotes PocketBase. |
| **Directus Data Studio** | Strong; the best admin app of the alternatives | Yes, Import/Export panel (headers must match field names; imports do **not** trigger Flows — [#20284](https://github.com/directus/directus/issues/20284)) | A native **Directus Mobile** app exists (iOS/Android) | The only alternative that clears the phone bar — at the cost of §6.4's licence question |
| **Appwrite console** | Yes | **NOT VERIFIED** | NOT VERIFIED | Unknown |
| **Plain container** | Nothing — unless written | **~40 lines.** `parseMetrics`/`parseTags`/`parseGoals` in `core/tabular.ts` already parse exactly this, and are already unit-tested | Yes — it is his own UI, mobile-first at 390 px like the rest of the app | **The cheapest correct answer.** |

That last row deserves emphasis. A "paste your TSV here" textarea in Settings is
`text.trim().split('\n').map((l) => l.split('\t'))` handed to `saveConfig` —
`parseMetrics` already validates it. `tabular.ts` was written to be the canonical
row layout for *any* tabular backend, headers-driven so columns can be reordered;
it already does the work. Reusing it costs one screen-sized component, not 800
lines, and preserves the `generate.py` workflow **exactly** rather than replacing
it with 36 columns of forms nobody wants to fill in.

**Trap 1 verdict: this is no longer the veto ADR 0001 made it. PocketBase fails
it outright (no CSV import, explicitly non-mobile admin). Supabase Studio passes
on a laptop and is unproven on a phone. The plain container passes for ~40 lines
by reusing tested code.**

## 5. Trap 2 — the scheduled conditional push

Requirement (ADR 0002): a morning and an evening reminder, **only if the day is
unfilled**, which means the job needs the data. The 2026-09 amendment settled the
design — the server holds `lastFilled` and `pendingDays`, decides, and sends a
real notification, because Chrome's rule is absolute: *"You have to show a
notification when you receive a push message"*
([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/push-notifications-on-the-open-web),
checked 2026-09-13). `server/decide.js` implements that decision, it is pure, and
`tests/push.test.ts` covers it.

**Ranked by how much can go silently wrong — the only ranking that matters for a
feature whose failure mode is "nothing happens".**

**1. Plain container — `setInterval` in the same process (best).** One timer,
recomputing from `new Date()` each tick so drift cannot accumulate, calling the
existing `decide()` against the same SQLite the app writes. No network hop, no
second service, no scheduler to misconfigure. It fails if the process dies —
`restart: unless-stopped` brings it back, and nothing is lost because the
decision is recomputed from the database — or if the container runs but wedges.
That second case earns one honest caveat: **plain Docker does not restart a
container because its `HEALTHCHECK` says unhealthy**; it flips the status label,
and the restart policy fires on process *exit* (Swarm is the one that reschedules
unhealthy tasks — see §12). Mitigation is one row: show `lastTickAt` in Settings,
so a stopped clock is visible the next time he opens the app.

**2. PocketBase — and this is where it stops being one container.** It has a cron
(`app.Cron()` in Go, `cronAdd()` in JS hooks) and `$http.send()`. But VAPID Web
Push needs **ECDSA-P256** signing and **ECDH + HKDF** for RFC 8291 `aes128gcm`
payload encryption, and the JS VM's `$security` module exposes only HMAC, SHA/MD5,
AES `encrypt`/`decrypt`, constant-time `equal` and random strings — **no
elliptic-curve primitives at all** ([jsvm/_security](https://pocketbase.io/jsvm/modules/_security.html),
checked 2026-09-13). It is a goja ES5 VM, not Node, so npm crypto packages do not
fill the gap; community implementations use Go's `webpush-go` **from a custom Go
binary**. So: (a) build and maintain a Go binary, losing "just pull the official
image", or (b) run the existing 464-line Node service as a **second container** —
at which point the count is two, and one container that does both is hard to
argue against.

**3. Supabase — `pg_cron` → `pg_net` → Edge Function → Web Push (worst).** Four
hops, each able to fail quietly. `pg_cron`'s availability is **never broken down
by plan** in the docs (inferring "yes on Free" is an inference, NOT VERIFIED).
`pg_net` is fire-and-forget: a failed call leaves a row in `net._http_response`
nobody reads. Edge Functions are Deno and the docs say *"cold starts are
possible."* **Supabase's own official push guide is FCM-based** — following the
documented path **re-Googles the deployment**, which is the requirement being
removed. Standards-based Web Push from Deno means `jsr:@negrel/webpush`, a
third-party library, because `npm:web-push` under Deno has a documented history of
AES-GCM breakage ([deno#23693](https://github.com/denoland/deno/issues/23693),
fixed in Deno; whether the Supabase Edge Runtime carries the fix is NOT VERIFIED).
And on Free, a paused project stops the cron.

Against all that, `web-push` on npm — already in `server/` — is v3.6.7, five
dependencies, with a commit **two days before this check**.

**Trap 2 verdict: the option that changes the least wins, and it is not close.
The BaaS route replaces a tested pure function plus one dependency with four
services, a third-party Deno library, and a documented default path that leads
back to Google.**

## 6. The options

### 6.1 Supabase Cloud (Free, and Pro at $25/mo)

Postgres, PostgREST, GoTrue, RLS, Storage, Deno Edge Functions, `pg_cron`. Free:
500 MB DB, 5 GB egress, 50 000 MAU, **max 2 active projects**, paused after 7 days
of low activity. Pro $25/mo: 8 GB, 250 GB egress, 7-day backups. Team $599. EU
regions pinned at creation (eu-west-1/2/3, eu-central-1/2, eu-north-1) with a DPA
and SCCs published; **no Free-tier region restriction found, and no confirmation
of parity either** ([pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing),
[regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions),
[dpa](https://supabase.com/legal/dpa), checked 2026-09-13).

**Good:** the best auth on the list, zero infrastructure to operate, a real table
editor with CSV import, not Google, and 500 MB against a 9 MB decade never binds.
`supabase-js` is ~25 kB gzipped (secondary source, §12) and can be skipped —
PostgREST is `fetch` with a bearer token, which suits a two-dependency project.

**Bad:** answers one of his five questions; introduces a public `anon` key; owns
§5's worst push pipeline; gives *no* backups on Free. And the strategic
objection: **it satisfies "less Google" and "someone else's problem" while
quietly abandoning "a Docker image on my own server" — the thing he asked for.**

### 6.2 Supabase self-hosted

The same API surface on his box. The official `docker compose` starts **seven
core services** — Studio, Kong/Envoy gateway, GoTrue, PostgREST, Realtime,
Storage, Postgres — plus optional Logflare and Vector. Stated minimum **4 GB RAM**,
8 GB+ recommended. No automatic backups, no PITR, manual upgrades, manual TLS
([self-hosting/docker](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting/docker),
checked 2026-09-13).

**The worst option in the document, and not a close call.** It fails N2 — that is
not "less infra", it is nine times the infra of the build he rejected — fails N4,
and keeps only the auth win, which PocketBase and a Caddy line also deliver at
one container and zero respectively. Nine services and 4 GB of RAM to store 9 MB
of text for one person is the exact shape of decision `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`
exists to prevent.

### 6.3 PocketBase

One MIT-licensed Go binary (~15 MB), embedded SQLite, auth, admin UI, realtime,
file storage, REST. v0.40.4, released **2026-09-12** — the day before this check —
61 025 stars. Still pre-1.0, and the docs say so: *"full backward compatibility is
not guaranteed before reaching v1.0.0."* Production guidance is systemd with
`Restart=always`, behind a reverse proxy. No official managed cloud; PocketHost
($9.99/mo) and an explicitly unaffiliated "PocketBase Cloud" exist.

On paper this answers his previous message and this one at once: one container,
self-hosted, no Google, with auth and an admin table editor he does not have to
write. **On inspection it fails both traps** — no record-level CSV import (closed
as not planned), an admin UI explicitly not going to be mobile-responsive (§4),
and Web Push unreachable from its JS hooks for want of EC crypto, so push needs a
custom Go binary or a second container (§5).

What survives is real: **excellent auth** (30 OAuth providers, MFA, a built-in
rate limiter) and **the best free backup story here** (scheduled ZIPs to S3,
restore from the UI). They are also, respectively, one Caddy line and five lines
of cron away in §6.6.

One more tension worth naming: to get value from the admin UI the collections
need typed fields matching `HEADERS`, so the pipe-separated lists, the `order`
integer and the "empty `active` means true" convention surface as raw text boxes
anyway. **For this data, PocketBase's admin UI is a worse Google Sheets that also
does auth.**

### 6.4 Appwrite, Nhost, Directus

**Appwrite** — Cloud Free ($0, 75 K MAU, 2 projects), Pro from $25/mo with daily
backups and 7-day retention, SLA at Enterprise only. Self-hosted brings up
**~15–20 containers** (approximate; no official single figure), 2 GB RAM minimum
and 4–8 GB realistically. Good auth, scheduled functions down to every minute,
a console. **CSV import: NOT VERIFIED.** Strictly dominated: it is Supabase's
profile with a heavier self-hosted footprint and a less verifiable admin story.

**Nhost** — Postgres + Hasura GraphQL + auth + storage + functions. Free $0 with
**a project paused after a week idle** (the same cliff as Supabase); Pro from
$25/mo with backups and PITR; Team $599 adds an email-support SLA. Actively
maintained as far as could be checked. Its distinguishing feature is GraphQL — a
query layer for a client that already loads everything in one round trip. **Pure
cost here, zero benefit.** Out.

**Directus** — genuinely the best *admin app* of the three, on a database he owns,
and it supports **SQLite**. Data Studio has CSV import and there is a **native
mobile app**: the only alternative that clears §4's phone bar. Two objections.
Licensing moved in **v12 (May 2026)** from BSL 1.1 to the Monospace Sustainable
Core License (GPLv3 after four years, free under $5 M revenue and 50 employees —
he qualifies comfortably), and a licence that changed structurally this year will
change again. More decisively, Directus is a data platform to put *in front of* a
database: running it means Directus **plus** the API it serves **plus** the push
job — trap 1 solved well, trap 2 not at all, at a container count well above one.
Remember it if trap 1 ever becomes the dominant constraint; not the answer today.

### 6.5 Status quo — Google Sheets

Still the best option in this document on four of the eight drivers: €0 with no
cliff, nothing to operate, free automatic versioning (§3.3), free backups and
export (§3.2), the best table editor in existence, and it works on his phone.

**It loses on one thing: D3, stated as a requirement** — and precision matters
here. Sheets is not being dropped because it strained, because goals outgrew it,
or because a spreadsheet is an undignified database; ADR 0001's numbers on all of
that still stand. It is being dropped because the owner said to stop using
Google, and an ADR that ignores a changed requirement is worth nothing.

What leaving costs, in one sentence anyone proposing it should say out loud
first: **free row-level history, free backups, a best-in-class editor, and zero
operations.**

### 6.6 The plain self-hosted container (Node + `node:sqlite`)

One `node:24-alpine` image on his server, behind his Caddy: the static build, a
REST API implementing the nine `HabitRepository` methods over SQLite, the
existing `decide.js` on a `setInterval`, and `web-push`. One dependency. Data in
one file he can `scp`.

**The honest marks against it, none of them fatal:**

- **`node:sqlite` is still experimental** — stability **1.2 "Release Candidate"**:
  *"hopefully ready to become stable. No further breaking changes are anticipated
  but may still occur"* ([nodejs.org/api/sqlite](https://nodejs.org/api/sqlite.html),
  checked 2026-09-13). Added v22.5.0, unflagged v22.13/v23.4, RC v25.7.0. It has
  prepared statements, WAL via `PRAGMA`, and a module-level `sqlite.backup()`.
  Fallback if it churns: `better-sqlite3`, at the cost of a native build.
- **`.nvmrc` says 20, and Node 20 is EOL.** Active LTS is **v24.21.0**. The bump
  is due anyway.
- **Auth is not included** — §3.5: one Caddy line, or ~100 reviewable lines.
- **He is the SLA, the backup operator and the patch cadence.** True, and equally
  true of PocketBase and self-hosted Supabase; §3.1 establishes the SLA is worth
  nothing here.

**What it wins:** trap 1 for ~40 lines of already-tested parsing, trap 2 by
changing nothing, the fewest daemons, no public key, no vendor, no free-tier
cliff, no pause policy — and the `HabitRepository` seam doing exactly what it was
built for, with `localRepository.ts` (157 lines) as the template.

## 8. Decision

**No. Do not move to a Backend-as-a-Service. Build the plain self-hosted
container (Node + `node:sqlite` + the existing `server/decide.js`), and solve
authentication at the reverse proxy or with a small, reviewed amount of stdlib
code — not by adopting a platform to get it.**

Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **A BaaS answers one of his five questions** — auth, completely and well. It
   answers "no" to SLA and versioning, "no unless you pay" to backups, and on
   security trades a model with no long-lived credentials for one built around a
   published key. One out of five is not a platform decision.
2. **Auth is available without the platform.** One `basic_auth` directive, bcrypt,
   ten minutes, zero lines — which removes the strongest argument for a BaaS from
   the board, and should be applied on day one whatever is built behind it.
3. **Trap 2 decides it.** ADR 0002's push is written, pure and tested. Supabase
   replaces it with `pg_cron` → `pg_net` → a cold-starting Deno function → a
   third-party web-push library whose documented happy path is **FCM, i.e.
   Google**: more infrastructure and more silent failure modes, in the name of
   less infrastructure.
4. **Trap 1 does not differentiate.** The workflow is generate-and-paste TSV;
   `core/tabular.ts` already parses it and a paste box is ~40 lines. PocketBase
   cannot import CSV at all and will not be mobile; Studio can, on a laptop.
5. **RLS is solving a problem he does not have.** One user; the isolation it
   provides is between him and nobody.
6. **Container count is the ops metric that predicts incidents.** One beats seven
   to nine, and self-hosted Supabase directly contradicts *« j'ai pas envie de
   configurer autant d'infra »*.

**Do these three things regardless of the above, because they are cheap and each
answers one of his questions outright:**

- **`git init` in `perso/`** (or un-ignore it into a private repo). That is the
  versioning answer for the only table where versioning matters (§3.3). Zero
  application code, backend-independent.
- **`basic_auth` in the Caddyfile** before anything is exposed. That is the auth
  answer (§3.5).
- **A nightly `VACUUM INTO` to off-box storage** — five lines. That is the backup
  answer (§3.2), with retention he chooses rather than 7 days for $300/year.

## 9. Consequences

### 9.1 Good

- **One container, one dependency, one data file.** `sqlite3 habits.db .dump` is
  the disaster-recovery plan; `VACUUM INTO` is the nightly one.
- **The push service survives unchanged.** `server/decide.js` is pure and tested;
  it moves from Cloud Run to a `setInterval` in the same process. The 418 lines of
  Terraform, plus Cloud Scheduler, GCS and Secret Manager, go away — N2, answered.
- **No Google in the data path.** N3, answered. (The push *transport* still hits
  `fcm.googleapis.com` for Chrome endpoints: that is the browser's choice of push
  service, carries an RFC 8291-encrypted payload Google cannot read, and is not a
  Google account, credential or service he configures.)
- **No public key, no free-tier cliff, no pause policy, no vendor pricing page.**
- **The seam did its job:** one new adapter, one branch in `src/lib/backend.ts`,
  nothing in `src/core/`, no screen touched — what `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` promises.
- **Bulk TSV import keeps `generate.py` working**, reusing parsers that have tests.

### 9.2 Bad — accepted with eyes open

- **He becomes the SLA, the backup operator and the patch cadence.** §3.1 says the
  SLA is worth nothing at one form a day; the other two are real and recurring, at
  an estimated 2–3 hours a year.
- **Row-level history is lost** relative to Google Sheets, except for `Config`,
  which git covers. History on notes or entries later is a 20-line SQLite trigger.
- **`node:sqlite` is a Release Candidate**, and requires moving off the EOL Node 20
  in `.nvmrc`. Escape hatch: `better-sqlite3`.
- **HTTP Basic is ugly:** no logout, no French error copy, a browser dialog rather
  than a screen. Replacing it costs ~100 lines — and that is the moment to re-read
  §3.5's first honest note.
- **Nobody else patches GoTrue-grade auth for him.** The genuine risk accepted
  here, accepted only because the Caddy option means there is no hand-written auth
  code on the internet at all.
- **A silently wedged container sends no reminders**, and Docker will not notice.
  Mitigation: `lastTickAt` in Settings (§5).
- **A second human would invalidate most of this**, as ADR 0002 §A.5 warns.

## 10. The runner-up, and the single question that separates them

**Runner-up: Supabase Cloud, Free plan** — not PocketBase, which §4 and §5 demote
below the plain container on both traps despite its excellent auth and backups.

Supabase Cloud is the **only option in this document where durability and auth are
genuinely somebody else's job**, which is constraint N4 stated in full. It wins
if N4 beats N3.

**The one question that separates them:**

> **Do I want to operate a machine at all — or did "a Docker image on my own
> server" mean "something simple", not "something I run"?**

If the answer is "I do not want to run a box", the plain container is the wrong
recommendation no matter how small: a box is a disk that fills, a cert that
renews, a base image with CVEs, and a process that can be down while he is abroad.
Take **Supabase Cloud Free** instead, cron a weekly `supabase db dump`, accept
that there is no SLA and no row history, keep `git` for `perso/`, and **keep the
push service as a tiny container anyway rather than porting it to `pg_cron` +
Deno** — §5 holds regardless of where the data lives.

**The cheapest way to answer it is not a spike, it is a lookup:** is the server he
has in mind *already* a machine he backs up and patches for something else? One
command against his backup target, five minutes. If yes, the marginal ops cost of
this app is near zero and the plain container wins outright. If no, he is about to
become the sole operator of a decade of his own health data on an unbackuped box —
a worse durability story than either Google Sheets or Supabase-Free-plus-a-dump —
and Supabase Cloud wins.

Do not spend a week on it: **the Caddy `basic_auth` line and the `git init` are
correct under both branches and cost fifteen minutes together.** Do them first;
they retire two of his five questions before the backend is even chosen.

## 11. Revisit triggers

Checkable, not vibes.

1. **A second human needs an account.** Everything inverts: RLS stops being moot,
   auth stops being a Caddy line, and ADR 0002 §A.5 must be honoured first.
   Supabase becomes the right answer rather than a considered one.
2. **He loses data once** — the disk dies and the restore does not work. Then
   durability was worth paying for: Supabase Pro, PocketHost, any managed store.
3. **Two hours in one year go to operating the box**, or he notices he is avoiding
   an upgrade. The self-hosting assumption was wrong; §10's runner-up takes over.
4. **HTTP Basic proves unusable on the installed PWA.** Adopt somebody's auth —
   PocketBase's or Supabase's — rather than writing it.
5. **`node:sqlite` ships a breaking change that costs an afternoon.** Move to
   `better-sqlite3`; do not move backend.
6. **Reminders stop arriving and nobody notices for a week.** §5's mitigation was
   insufficient; add a real liveness check before adding a platform.
7. **A feature needs a query that cannot be answered in memory.** Unchanged from
   ADR 0001 §8; nothing on the roadmap trips it.

**Explicitly not a trigger:** a nicer free tier; row count; the goals feature;
"a spreadsheet/SQLite file feels undignified"; wanting RLS; or a Supabase blog
post.
